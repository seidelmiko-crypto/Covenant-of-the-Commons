## PREAMBLE

The revolution must pay its own way, or it is a charity case waiting for a patron with terms. This Document constitutes the commercial engine: a **regenerative hemp consortium — seed to product** — designed as a self-sustaining economic ecosystem network whose profits flow, by constitutional requirement, to the mission. The business is the revolution; here is the business.

The thesis: industrial hemp is the rare industry that is simultaneously **lawful, scalable, vertically integrable, regenerative by agronomy, and morally aligned** — a crop that rebuilds soil, sequesters carbon, feeds, clothes, shelters, and heals, in a market still fragmented enough for a disciplined entrant to build the integrator rather than fight one. We do not enter this industry to participate in it. We enter to prove the model that funds everything else — and to prove it under audit, or not at all.

## TITLE I — THE CONSORTIUM: SEED TO PRODUCT

**§1.1 Legal Form.** The engine operates as a **consortium of operating companies** coordinated under the commercial holding layer: the **Burgeon Consortium, PBC** (Doc X Title III) — a Delaware public benefit corporation with the Trust holding a mission-veto class share, the Dutch stichting-and-company pattern (foundation above trade). The conglomerate is the business; the Trust is its conscience; the Ledger is its testimony.

**§1.2 The Value Chain — Seven Integrated Segments.**

| Segment | Operation | Products / Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 Genetics & Seed | Certified seed production, cultivar stewardship | Seed sales, licensing |
| E-2 Cultivation Network | Contracted regenerative farms; cooperative grower network | Biomass, grain, fiber contracts |
| E-3 Primary Processing | **Mobile decortication first (Doc XXII): trailer-mounted toll processing that goes to the crop**; stationary micro-hub only where toll volume proves it | Processing tolls; fiber/hurd/grain streams |
| E-4 Fiber & Materials | Textiles, nonwovens, biocomposites, hempcrete and building materials | B2B materials; construction products |
| E-5 Grain & Food | Hearts, protein, oil — food-grade, FDA-compliant | Consumer food brands; ingredients |
| E-6 Wellness & Extracts | **EXCLUDED from founding scope** (Doc IX §9.3): deferred until E-3/E-4 unit economics are proven and counsel clears the regulatory posture; revisited no earlier than year three | — |
| E-7 Carbon & Ecological Services | **The biochar/carbon stack (Doc XXII Part III):** mobile pyrolysis of hurd and residues; verified regenerative-practice documentation; credits only under recognized third-party methodology with measurement, verification, and public registry listing | Biochar product; verified carbon-removal credits |

**§1.3 The Integrator Thesis (recalculated).** The industry's failure mode is fragmentation: farmers without processors, processors without supply, brands without supply chains. The design answer is **vertical integration through cooperation, executed mobile-first**: own the bottleneck as a *service* — toll processing that travels to the crop — coordinate the rest through covenant-style grower and buyer networks. Whoever serves the bottleneck sets the industry's tempo; the mobile unit is the bottleneck served (Doc XXII Part I).

**§1.4 Regenerative Standard.** All E-2 cultivation operates under a published Regenerative Protocol: soil-building rotations, minimal tillage, no persistent synthetics, third-party verified — U-1's open-methodology discipline applied to agricultural claims. The regenerative claim is falsifiable and ledger-registered, or it is not made. **The carbon-integrity rule is registered: the institution that sells verification cannot sell unverified carbon (Doc XXII Part III).**

**§1.5 Legal Status (as of publication).** Federal hemp regulation is in transition: the 2018 Farm Bill framework currently applies (7 U.S.C. § 1639o et seq.), and amendments enacted in Public Law 119-37 are scheduled to change the federal definition effective 12 November 2026, including THC measurement. All licensing and equipment decisions are made against the law in force at the time of execution, together with applicable USDA, state, tribal, product, environmental, and local requirements.

## TITLE II — THE REGENERATIVE ECONOMIC ECOSYSTEM NETWORK

**§2.1 Self-Sustaining Design.** The engine is architected as a closed-loop regional ecosystem, replicable per Zone: farms feed processing; processing feeds manufacturing; manufacturing feeds markets; profits feed mission and the next region; residues return to soil, materials, and the carbon stack. Each regional loop targets local self-sufficiency in essential segments within five years.

**§2.2 Zone Integration.** The engine is the economic content of the Zone system (Doc III, Title IV): founding operations in Z-1 Anchor Zones; licensed replication in Z-2 Covenant Zones; and in Z-5 Frontier Zones the **mobile unit is the transitional-development instrument itself** — regenerative processing as the first legitimate economy of emergent administrations (the operationalization of the self-assessment's strongest finding, Doc VII Part III.3).

**§2.3 The Proof Obligation (stated as testable objective).** The engine exists to prove that mission-locked capital outperforms extractive capital on a long horizon. Therefore: books ledger-registered; margins, wages, ecological outcomes, and failure thresholds published; bankruptcy protocol real. The registered objective: *to demonstrate, under independent audit against published benchmarks over a five-year horizon, whether mission-locked capital can achieve risk-adjusted returns comparable to conventional operators in the same segments* (Doc XIX §7). If the engine fails, the failure will be the most honest document in the industry.

## TITLE III — THE SEVEN BODIES: ONE ORGANIZATION, SEVEN FORMS

The decentralized organization *is*, simultaneously, the business, the civil service, the civil society, the benevolent order, the workers' coalition, the political movement — the revolution as a whole. This Title constitutes those bodies and their firewalls.

**§3.1 The Conglomerate (Commercial Body).** The hemp consortium and successors under Burgeon. Purpose: profit in service of mission.

**§3.2 The Civil Service Infrastructure (Administrative Body).** The Covenant Civil Service Corps (Doc III, Title V) — at formation, a corps of one, practicing commercially through the bootstrap agency (Doc XXII §2).

**§3.3 The Civil Society Organization (Civic Body).** The Civic Congregations (Doc XXV) and the open civic layer — education, community, public methodology. Hosts: 501(c)(3) and Verein.

**§3.4 The Philanthropic Benevolent Order (Charitable Body).** The Renewal Service and mission programs — including the standing rule that 10% of bootstrap-agency hours are pro bono, ledger-registered (Doc XXII Part V). Strictly nonpartisan.

**§3.5 The Fraternal Workers Coalition (Labor Body).** The Covenant Workers Coalition: wage floors, profit participation, Academy access, mutual-aid funds, lodge structure; worker equity targeted at 20–30% over ten years, final vehicle (ESOP-style trust or equivalent) determined through tax and legal structuring in the pilot jurisdiction — a target, not a selected mechanism (Doc XIX §7). **Lodge No. 1 is chartered with the first machine — the first equipment operator is hired under Coalition terms (Doc XXII Stage 3).** The Coalition holds defined governance voice in consortium labor decisions.

**§3.6 The Political Machine and Movement (Civic-Advocacy Body).** Advocacy for the policy horizon — regenerative agriculture, hemp normalization, statelessness reform, verification standards, covenant standing. **CRITICAL FIREWALL:** partisan electoral activity and lobbying beyond 501(c)(3) limits are housed in a separate 501(c)(4) Movement Vehicle — never the charitable arm, never Trust mission capital, never commingled with Coalition dues (Doc IX §8; implemented at human scale by the Congregations' Two-Vessel Rule, Doc XXV §1.4).

**§3.7 The Revolution (Integrating Body).** The revolution is the *state of coherence among the seven*: business feeds workers; workers staff the civil service; the civil service delivers the benevolence; benevolence builds civil society; civil society legitimizes the movement; the movement protects the conditions in which business operates. When any body feeds on the others, it is a racket. The Ledger distinguishes.

## TITLE IV — CAPITAL, GOVERNANCE, AND THE TRUST

**§4.1 Capital Doctrine.** Trust covenants bind the conglomerate: no extraction distributions; defined reinvestment ratios; wage and ecological floors as conditions of capital release; Trust mission veto over conglomerate decisions touching mission integrity.

**§4.2 Revenue Allocation (Registered).** Consortium distributable surplus — defined as after-tax profit remaining after reserves and debt service — allocated: **50% reinvestment · 20% Workers Coalition participation · 20% mission via the Trust · 10% Zone development.** Applies across consolidated subsidiaries; losses and liquidity shortfalls suspend distributions in reverse order (community, mission, worker) with mandatory disclosure; amendment only by constitutional process; never below 20% mission (Doc IX §9.2, Doc XIX §7).

**§4.3 Governance Voice.** Consortium governance includes: Trust representative (mission), Workers Coalition representative (labor), Zone representative where operations are sited (community), commercial management (competence). No single constituency controls; the Trust vetoes only on mission grounds.

**§4.4 Investor Doctrine.** Outside capital buys economics, never mission governance; no share class carries Anchor powers; offering documents disclose the Trust veto, surplus ratios, and exit/fork rights. Capital that cannot accept the mission lock is capital the Consortium does not take (Doc X §3.3).

## TITLE V — RISK AND HONEST ASSESSMENT (REGISTERED)

1. **Agricultural-market risk (IA-10).** The 2019 oversupply collapse is the registered cautionary pattern (treated as planning assumption pending the pilot's published market analysis, per Prospectus v1.2 Note 6). Mitigations: processing-first as *service*; cultivation contract-capped to commissioned processing capacity (hardened cap: ≤120%, amendment requires Guardian-level concurrence plus published rationale — Doc IX §9.5); diversified streams E-3 through E-7.
2. **Regulatory risk.** Licensing variance; FDA posture (E-6 excluded); banking/insurance friction; the 12 November 2026 federal transition (§1.5). Compliance is a founding department, not a retrofit.
3. **Mobile-unit throughput and seasonality** (Doc XXII Part VI): toll pricing must reflect real throughput; the services agency is the counter-seasonal revenue.
4. **Carbon-market integrity risk:** voluntary markets face credibility scrutiny; the third-party-verified-or-nothing rule is existential for this institution above all others.
5. **Reputational coupling.** The engine's failures are the mission's failures — the Proof Obligation's point and its danger. Siting, contracting, and product claims operate under U-1 verification discipline.
6. **Single-founder bandwidth** (Stages 0–2): the binding constraint; staging exists to buy the first hire with earned revenue.
7. **Dilution risk:** the bootstrap agency succeeding so well it becomes the business — countered by mission-lock surplus routing from the first dollar (Doc XXII Part VI).
