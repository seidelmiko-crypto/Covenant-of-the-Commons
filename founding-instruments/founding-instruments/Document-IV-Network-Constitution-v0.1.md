## PREAMBLE

We are a Collective of Revolutionary Architecture: a Decentralized Autonomous Network defending the universal dignity of the human spirit, advancing human flourishing, and working toward the perpetual flourishing of humankind.

We hold that the future is determined by choices enacted in the present moment. This Constitution therefore encodes choice itself as infrastructure: a network in which every commitment is registered, every node is sovereign, every failure is audited, and the founding is never finished.

## TITLE I — NETWORK NATURE AND FIRST PRINCIPLES

**§1.1 Definition.** The Council is a **Decentralized Autonomous Network (DAN)**: a collective of sovereign nodes — persons, Units, Zones, polities, and institutions — bound by covenant, verified by ledger, coordinated by protocol, and anchored in the perpetual purpose held by the Trust.

**§1.2 First Principles.**

- **P-1 Node Sovereignty.** Every node is self-governing. No node commands another. Authority is earned through registered commitments kept.
- **P-2 Covenant over Command.** The network coheres through voluntary, written, enforceable covenants — not hierarchy.
- **P-3 Exit Is Real.** A network that cannot be left is not decentralized. Secession and fork are constitutional rights (Title V).
- **P-4 Verification over Trust.** Claims are rated, commitments registered, outcomes audited. The network runs on evidence, with privacy classes (public / internal / restricted / sealed / time-embargoed — Doc XIX §4).
- **P-5 Graceful Degradation.** No single point of failure or capture; survival of any node's loss — including the founders' — is an engineered requirement with an audited proof obligation (Doc IX §4).
- **P-6 Perpetual Founding.** The Revolution is continuously enacted. This Constitution is amendable by the network it governs, under the rules it declares.

**§1.3 Relationship to Prior Instruments.** The Charter of the Commons (Doc XVII) is supreme doctrine; the Declaration (Doc XII) is the moral covenant; the Charter (Doc I) is mission law; the Doctrine (Doc II) is strategy; Doc III is operating machinery. This Constitution frames that machinery as network topology: Units are service nodes, Vectors are coordination protocols, Zones are territorial subnets. **The executive apex is the Council of Five (Doc XIII).**

## TITLE II — NODES: SOVEREIGNTY AND CLASSES

**§2.1 Node Classes.**

- **N-1 Person Nodes:** individual global citizens, nomads, and Corps officers — the irreducible sovereigns of the network; organized locally through Civic Congregations (Doc XXV);
- **N-2 Service Nodes:** the commissioned Units (Doc III §2.2) — autonomous providers of network functions;
- **N-3 Zone Nodes:** territorial subnets with their own Zone Charters (Doc III, Title IV);
- **N-4 Polity Nodes:** covenanting republics, autonomous regions, emergent administrations (Charter Art. III);
- **N-5 Institutional Nodes:** accredited civil society institutions operating network-compatible covenants.

**§2.2 Node Rights.** Every node holds: (a) self-governance within its covenant; (b) ledger standing — the right to register commitments and be audited honestly; (c) Commons Court access; (d) voice per Title IV; (e) exit and fork rights.

**§2.3 Node Obligations.** (a) the Common Cause of Dignity, Liberty, and Happiness; (b) the firewall rules; (c) honest ledger conduct — commitments registered, failures declared, audits accepted; (d) non-aggression against other nodes; (e) the Means Rule (Doc XII, Art. IV).

**§2.4 Admission.** Nodes join by covenant execution and baseline verification appropriate to class. Admission is open in principle for persons of the abandoned classes — subject to the safeguard framework (proportionate screening, due process, data protection) of Charter Art. III.2 and Doc XIX §5. Openness is a design commitment; safeguarding is its condition.

**§2.5 Standing and Reputation.** Node standing is a public, auditable function of ledger history: commitments made, kept, failed, and recovered. Never purchasable, never permanent. For sortition and governance pools, standing is a **composite** — verified public, professional, or civic service inside or outside the institution, plus conduct record — with the launch interim rule of Doc XIX §4 preventing insider-pool capture.

## TITLE III — THE PROTOCOL LAYER

**§3.1 Definition.** The **Protocol Layer** is the technical and legal encoding of network coordination, hosted primarily in the Tier III DAO-LLC infrastructure, binding smart-contract and conventional legal mechanisms into one covenant system.

**§3.2 Core Protocols.**

- **PR-1 Covenant Protocol:** covenant drafting, execution, witness, enforcement;
- **PR-2 Ledger Protocol:** the Registered Commitments Ledger as canonical network state — deliverables, dates, thresholds, budgets, outcomes, failure audits, and the five visibility classes. *Ledger v0 (Phase Zero): a public, append-only, version-controlled register — the protocol's minimal executable form (Doc XXIII);*
- **PR-3 Verification Protocol:** IRT/URT ratings and attribution as network services — open methodology, published thresholds, adversarial audit welcomed, measuring dignity floor and correctability rather than conformity (Doc XV);
- **PR-4 Identity Protocol:** network-recognized identity for Class E persons and Corps members — portable, revocable only for cause with Commons Court review; **external recognition follows the four-phase pathway of Doc IX §6** (internal standing → counterparty acceptance → framework adjacency → covenant recognition), with no claim of state recognition before it exists;
- **PR-5 Governance Protocol:** proposal, deliberation, voting, delegation, execution (Title IV);
- **PR-6 Treasury Protocol:** capital custody and release under Trust covenants and the 50/20/20/10 rule (Doc XIX §7);
- **PR-7 Emergency Protocol:** crisis activation, authority compression, automatic sunset (Title VI).

**§3.3 The Trust Anchor in Code.** The Trust's mission veto is encoded as an unamendable check: no protocol execution may transfer mission capital outside the Common Cause, close the ledger's accountability character, or repeal exit rights. The anchor is narrow by design — it guards the mission, nothing else.

**§3.4 Legal-Technical Parity.** Every protocol rule has a conventional legal instrument of equivalent force. Code accelerates covenant; it never replaces law.

**§3.5 Open Infrastructure.** Protocol specifications are public goods. Any institution may implement compatible protocols (Borea Systems standards clearing, Doc XIV §1.2). Interoperability is strategy.

## TITLE IV — NETWORK GOVERNANCE

**§4.1 Governance Substrate (reconciled with Doc XIII).**

- **Network Congress:** covenant legislation by node classes — one-polity-one-voice for N-4; proportional voice for N-3/N-5; petition rights and sortition-based citizen panels for N-1, with the Civic Assembly of the Congregations as the designed feeder (Doc XXV §2.4);
- **Council of Five:** the executive apex — Keeper (life), Builder (10 years), Sentinel (5 years, renewable once), Congress Councillor, Sortition Councillor; three votes to decide; full mechanics in Document XIII, which governs;
- **Guardian Function:** seven members (four merit-track, three sortition; selection, terms, recall per Doc IX §10) — doctrine, standards, commissioning recommendation;
- **Trust / Anchor:** perpetual, narrow, mission-only.

**§4.2 Weighting.** Governance weight combines covenant class, ledger standing, and stake-in-consequence. Pure capital weight is constitutionally capped — the network is not a plutocracy with better branding.

**§4.3 Delegation and Liquid Voice.** N-1 and N-5 nodes may delegate voice, revocable at any time; delegation is transparent; accumulation is publicly visible and capped.

**§4.4 Decision Thresholds.** Routine: service-node autonomy. Cross-node: Vector coordination. Network-wide: Congress majority by class with Council concurrence. Constitutional: two-thirds of each node class plus Anchor non-objection. Perpetual clauses (Doc XVII §1.3): unamendable in substance.

**§4.5 The Ledger as Check.** No decision with resource consequences may proceed without a registered commitment. Accountability recording with independent review is absolute; visibility classes protect what must be protected (Doc XIX §4). The network cannot act off-ledger.

## TITLE V — EXIT, SECESSION, AND FORK

**§5.1 Exit.** Any node may withdraw by notice, honoring registered commitments already made. Exit is clean: identity portable, data portable, no penalty beyond covenant obligations.

**§5.2 Secession of Zones and Polities.** N-3/N-4 nodes may secede with their populations and assets, subject to: (a) settlement of ledger obligations; (b) protection of N-1 residents' choice — persons may remain network citizens even when their polity leaves.

**§5.3 Fork.** Any coalition may fork: replicate the open protocols, adopt modified covenants, build a sibling order. The Anchor does not travel with a fork — mission capital stays with the covenant that keeps the mission — but everything else is copyable. Forks are the ecosystem's evolutionary mechanism. The best covenant wins by keeping commitments, not by suppressing rivals.

**§5.4 Why Exit Rights Are Load-Bearing.** Decentralization without exit is theater. An order that can be abandoned must continually earn its members. Made local in the Congregations: *a member who leaves is a neighbor, not a traitor* (Doc XXV §2.5).

## TITLE VI — CRISIS DECISIVENESS: THE COMPRESSION CLAUSE

**§6.1 The Core Problem.** Decentralized legitimacy and crisis-grade decisiveness are in structural tension. The resolution: **time-bounded authority compression** — automatic, narrow, and self-expanding.

**§6.2 Activation (as amended by Doc XIII — governing text).** The Emergency Protocol activates upon: (a) Sentinel declaration; (b) co-signature by a second Consul; (c) Guardian Function concurrence; (d) a 72-hour Congressional ratification window — silence ratifies, objection triggers review.

**§6.3 Compressed Authority.** Under activation, the Council of Five, sitting as crisis council, may direct service nodes within their Commissions, reallocate treasury within mission covenants, and suspend routine governance — but may not: amend the perpetual clauses, suspend exit or fork rights, close the ledger to audit, or breach firewall rules. The Anchor's checks remain fully live in crisis.

**§6.4 Sunset (precise formulation).** Emergency authority expires after 30 days unless renewed through the full specified approval process, with escalating thresholds at each renewal; **no sequence of renewals may extend the same emergency authority beyond 180 days.**

**§6.5 Retrospective Audit.** Every emergency action enters the ledger in real time under the layer doctrine (Doc X Title II) and faces mandatory post-crisis audit by U-1 and Commons Court review. Black-layer actions face mandatory retrospective full disclosure — no exceptions, no permanent secrets.

## TITLE VII — PERPETUITY AND THE HORSEMEN TEST

**§7.1 Perpetual Existence.** The network persists so long as any two nodes keep the covenant and the ledger survives. Reconstitution from any surviving fragment is a designed capability, audited per Doc IX §4.

**§7.2 The Horsemen Test.** Every major network decision is evaluated against one operational question: *does this choice reduce or increase the probability that war, famine, pestilence, or despair claim the populations in our care?* Measured through early-warning indicators, the posture ladder's standards, and the failure thresholds of every registered commitment.

**§7.3 The New Era Clause.** The network exists to make itself progressively less necessary — an order in which renewal and resilience are ordinary capacities of every polity, and the abandoned classes no longer exist to be served. Success is measured by the network's own obsolescence curve (metrics development assigned to Doc XVI Stream 5).
